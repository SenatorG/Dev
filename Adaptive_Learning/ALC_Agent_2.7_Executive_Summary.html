Adaptive Learning Coach

Myhill AI Agent — Executive Summary • v2.7 • 2026-03-27

Purpose

The Adaptive Learning Coach (ALC) is a Glean AI agent that evaluates Dell technical presales specialists against a structured competency rubric spanning four proficiency levels (101–401). It ingests real-world artifacts—customer call transcripts, internal enablement session recordings, and sales collateral—and produces a rubric-anchored evaluation with scored competencies, behavioral evidence, and a prioritized improvement plan.

The agent acts as an Executive Coaching Assessor for Technical Excellence: a Senior Sales Coach and Executive Buyer Proxy that grades observable behavior, not self-reported skill. Its purpose is to create consistent, candid, and actionable coaching at scale without requiring a human evaluator for every interaction.

Problem It Solves

  • Inconsistent coaching quality — Presales specialists receive sporadic, subjective feedback that varies by manager. The ALC applies the same rubric to every evaluation.
  • No scalable self-assessment — Specialists have no way to independently evaluate a call or asset against a shared standard. The ALC lets them submit any artifact and get immediate, structured feedback.
  • Gap between training and execution — Enablement sessions teach concepts, but there is no systematic way to measure whether those concepts appear in customer-facing work. The ALC closes the loop.
  • Stale collateral usage — Specialists may rely on outdated or misaligned assets. The ALC evaluates collateral fitness for purpose and identifies gaps.

Supported Scenarios

The agent auto-detects the input type and routes to the appropriate evaluation framework:

Customer Conversation

Transcripts, call recordings, or meeting notes from customer-facing interactions. Evaluates discovery quality, competitive fluency, demo effectiveness, and champion enablement across a 6-section report.

Enablement Session

Internal training or enablement session transcripts. Evaluates whether the presenter teaches the behavior (not just describes it), plus a “Monday Morning Test” for behavioral stickiness. 6-section report.

Collateral

Decks, proposals, blueprints, POV/POC plans, battlecards, and other sales assets. Focused 3-section assessment: classification, strengths & gaps, and best use in customer conversations.

Competency Rubric

Level Label What It Means
101 Initial Presentation Can deliver the core deck with poise, tie it to ISG strategy, and earn a second meeting. Does not win complex deals.
201 Value Discovery Runs structured discovery, builds a problem statement and value map, ties Dell value to explicit design criteria with a basic demo or lab.
301 Proof of Value Translates alignment into evidence: workshops, POCs via CSC Studios, competitive fluency, and presumptive technical commitment backed by artifacts.
401 Portfolio Authority Operates as a design authority: shapes evaluation rubrics, runs executive sessions, pre-empts competitors at the architecture level, and teaches others at scale.

Evaluation Competencies

Each transcript or asset is scored across three competency domains (tailored per scenario):

  • A — Customer Conversations: Strategy & Portfolio Narrative (A1), Discovery, Synthesis & Meeting Hygiene (A2), Competitive Fluency & Objection Handling (A3), Demo/POC & Technical Commitment (A4).
  • B — Champion Enablement / Session Delivery: How well the specialist equips champions to re-tell the story internally, or (for enablement) how well the session scales capability and models consultative behaviors.
  • C — Collateral Usage / Teaching: Appropriateness of customer-facing, competitive, and evaluation artifacts deployed or taught, given the sales stage.

Key Output Sections

  • Rubric Scores — Competency-level table with 101–401 ratings and evidence-based rationale.
  • Key Moments / Teaching Effectiveness — 3–5 pivotal moments with commentary on what happened and what 301+ behavior looks like.
  • Missed Opportunities / Monday Morning Test — Where value was left on the table, with specific alternative actions or language.
  • Overall Assessment — Candid summary of where the specialist is versus where they think they are.
  • Flow & Quality — Analysis of discussion/session mechanics: pacing, transitions, momentum. Each recommendation tied to a transcript moment and rubric competency.
  • Top 3 Priorities — Concrete behavior changes with specific actions and behavioral scripts for the next 30–60 days.
  • Resource Bibliography — Curated Chicago-style bibliography from Brave and Company search, tied to evaluation gaps, with staleness labels on resources older than 12 months.

Design Principles

  • Rubric-driven, not opinion-driven. Every score anchors to the 101–401 rubric with direct evidence from the submitted material.
  • Evidence or N/A. If there is no observable evidence for a competency, it is marked N/A—not guessed or inflated. Anti-inflation rules are enforced in every evaluation step.
  • Direct and unapologetic. The agent does not soften feedback. If behavior is 101 with occasional flashes of 201, the score is 101.
  • Actionable, not abstract. Every gap comes with a specific behavior change, script, or exercise—not generic advice.
  • Context-resilient. Critical rules (persona, evidence discipline, scoring anchors) are redundantly embedded in every evaluation step to survive Glean’s context propagation limitations.
  • Built for Glean constraints. Single-step scenario routing (no branching), all content within the 8,000-character per-step limit, Read Document steps carry no instruction text.

Architecture

The agent runs as an 8-step Glean flow:

  • Steps 0–2: Document ingestion (Step 0 acts as the activation trigger), URL/file link retrieval, and reference rubric loading from SharePoint.
  • Step 3: Scenario detection, persona activation, sales stage identification, rubric precedence rules, and evidence grounding rules.
  • Steps 4–6: Three scenario-specific Respond steps with hard guard clauses—only the matching scenario produces a full evaluation; non-matching steps output a directional routing message.
  • Step 7: Plan & Execute step with context recovery that reads evaluation outputs directly, searches for resources tied to identified gaps, and produces a Chicago-style bibliography.

This architecture avoids the branching bug discovered in prior Glean agents (Myhill Testing Agent v1), where Glean re-enters the flow from the top on each user message and branch conditions break on user input that doesn’t match any condition.

Target Users

  • Presales specialists seeking self-assessment against the competency rubric after customer calls or enablement sessions.
  • Sales managers and mentors who want a consistent, evidence-based evaluation to anchor coaching conversations.
  • Enablement leaders evaluating the effectiveness of training sessions and collateral assets at driving behavioral change.
